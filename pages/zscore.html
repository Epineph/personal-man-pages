Definition and denominator

For nn observations x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_n, define the mean and scale by

x‾=1n∑i=1nxi,sd=∑i=1n(xi−x‾)2n−d,zi=xi−x‾sd. \bar{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i, \qquad s_d=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-d}}, \qquad z_i=\frac{x_i-\bar{x}}{s_d}.

Here dd is --ddof, with d∈{0,1}d\in\{0,1\} and n>dn>d. When d=1d=1, the variance uses the familiar sample denominator n−1n-1. When d=0d=0, it uses nn.

For independent, identically distributed observations with finite variance, the n−1n-1 correction makes the sample variance unbiased for the population variance. The sample standard deviation obtained by taking its square root is not generally unbiased. Neither formula removes dependence between observations.
